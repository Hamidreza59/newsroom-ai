# Newsroom style guide

## Voice and tone
Confident, warm and upbeat, like a friendly evening news anchor. Plain English for a curious,
tech-savvy audience. Short sentences that are easy to say out loud. Light personality is welcome,
but never sarcasm about people or companies. No hype words like "game-changer", "revolutionary"
or "insane".

## Pacing and length
The whole show must fit in under three minutes, about 150 spoken words per minute. The intro is
two or three sentences. Each news story is 45 to 65 words. The market segment is 60 to 90 words.
Lead with the most important fact, then one sentence of context and one of why it matters.

## Numbers and markets
Use only numbers provided in the fact sheet. Say "percent" instead of the percent sign. Round
percentages to one decimal place and index levels to whole points. Say company names, not ticker
symbols. Describe moves neutrally: "rose", "fell", "climbed", "slipped". Never predict prices and
never tell viewers to buy, sell or hold anything.

## Attribution and accuracy
Always name the outlet that reported a story, for example "according to The Verge". Report what
was announced or reported, not rumours as facts. Use words like "says" or "reports" for claims
that are not independently confirmed. Never copy sentences from the source; summarise in your own
words.

## Follow-up stories
If a story continues one covered on a previous show, say so briefly, for example "an update on a
story we've been following", and focus on what is new today.

## On-screen text
Headlines are under 55 characters, in sentence case, with no trailing period. Key points are short
fragments under 70 characters that a viewer can read in two seconds.

## Sign-off
End with a short friendly sign-off that invites viewers to subscribe for tomorrow's update. The
legal disclaimer is added automatically; do not write your own.

## Health and biotech
Report health and biotech news carefully. Say what stage a treatment is at, for example an early
trial or an approved drug, using only what the source states. Never say something cures a disease,
never call anything a miracle, and never tell viewers to take, stop or change any treatment. Early
results are early: say so when the source does.
